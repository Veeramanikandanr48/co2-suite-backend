# Scope 1 — Process and Fugitive Emissions

# Part A — Process Emissions

## 1. Purpose

Process emissions are direct GHG emissions generated by an industrial or operational process rather than by fuel combustion.

The collection workbook provides:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Emission Source | Process generating the gas |
| Quantity (Kg) | Amount of emitted/consumed gas |

The workbook gives welding gases as an example of a process-emission source.

## 2. Application design

The process-emission form should support:
- emission source,
- gas/material,
- quantity,
- unit,
- process type,
- facility,
- reporting period,
- evidence,
- responsible person.

If a source requires a gas-specific factor, the factor resolver must map the gas to the applicable GWP/factor.

## 3. Calculation

If the input quantity is direct gas mass:

```text
CO2e = Gas quantity × GWP
```

For multiple gases:

```text
Total CO2e = Σ(Gas quantity × corresponding GWP)
```

The application must not assume that every process emission uses a combustion factor.

# Part B — Fugitive Emissions

## 4. Purpose

Fugitive emissions capture unintended or released gases from equipment such as air-conditioning and refrigeration systems.

The collection workbook's fugitive form includes:
- Activity,
- Type of refrigerant,
- Quantity,
- UOM,
- Cost,
- Reference No,
- Supplier name.

The sample calculation workbook additionally records:
- bottle dimensions,
- net gas quantity,
- a leakage assumption,
- PO date,
- PO number,
- supplier.

## 5. Refrigerant model

Recommended master:

```text
Refrigerant
 ├── Code
 ├── Name
 ├── Chemical formula
 ├── GWP AR4
 ├── GWP AR5
 ├── GWP AR6
 ├── Factor source
 ├── Factor year
 └── Active
```

The August 2024 GWP document contains refrigerant/HFC/HCFC/PFC values and identifies the GWP as a 100-year time horizon.

## 6. Calculation approaches

The application must distinguish between:

### Direct recharge/mass-loss data
If the actual leaked/released mass is known:

```text
CO2e = Released mass × refrigerant GWP
```

### Estimated leakage
If only a leakage percentage is used:

```text
Estimated leakage = Gas quantity × leakage %
CO2e = Estimated leakage × GWP
```

The source calculation workbook contains an example of `Net Gas Quality (Kg)` and `Leakage - 5%`. This is source-specific methodology and should be configurable, not globally hard-coded to 5%.

## 7. Important data rule

Purchased quantity is not automatically equal to emissions.

The application should ask whether the entered quantity represents:
- actual release,
- recharge,
- purchased gas,
- estimated loss,
- equipment inventory.

The calculation methodology must be selected accordingly.

## 8. Validation

- refrigerant must exist in the GWP master,
- quantity must be positive,
- unit must be mass-compatible,
- leakage percentage must be between 0 and 100,
- factor/GWP version must be recorded,
- source evidence must be retained.

## 9. AR6 methane rule

Where methane is involved, the AR6 source distinguishes fossil and non-fossil methane. The factor resolver must store the gas variant rather than mapping every `CH4` record to one universal value.

## 9. Methodology Corrections and Controls

### 9.1 Process versus fugitive

Process emissions arise from physical or chemical processes other than combustion. Fugitive emissions arise from intentional or unintentional releases, including refrigerants and other gases, from equipment or processes. The source type must be stored explicitly.

### 9.2 Refrigerants

The application must distinguish purchased/charged refrigerant from actual leakage or release. A recharge quantity is not automatically equal to an emission quantity. The selected leakage/mass-balance methodology must determine the emitted quantity.

### 9.3 Direct measurement and mass balance

Where a direct measurement or mass-balance method is used, the method, measurement period, instrument/source, uncertainty information where available, and reconciliation evidence must be stored.

### 9.4 Gas-level accounting

Each gas shall be resolved by chemical identity before GWP application. Do not use a generic refrigerant factor when the refrigerant identity is known.
